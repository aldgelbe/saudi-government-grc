Governance Workflow Architecture
Detailed process flows for critical governance activities — escalation, breach, approval, exceptions
🚨 Risk Escalation Workflow
1
Risk Detection & Identification

Operational unit identifies a risk breaching the defined KRI threshold or risk appetite boundary

2
Immediate Initial Assessment

Unit manager assesses impact and likelihood and classifies the severity level (P1–P4)

3
GRC Unit Notification

GRC Unit is notified within the prescribed timeframe corresponding to the risk severity level

4
Escalation Decision

GRC Unit determines: self-treat, escalate to Risk Committee, or escalate to Senior Leadership

5
Treatment Plan & Monitoring

Approved response plan, assigned owner, logged in Risk Register RSK, periodic monitoring initiated

⚖️ Compliance Breach Management Workflow
1
Breach Discovery

Identified through internal audit, obligations monitoring, whistleblowing channels, or regulatory bodies

2
Classification & Documentation

Classify breach (material / procedural) and document in Obligations Register OBL with full details

3
Root Cause Analysis

Apply 5-Why or Fishbone methodology to determine the underlying root cause

4
Corrective Action Plan

Approved remediation plan with designated owner, binding timeline, and control improvements

5
Regulatory Notification & Closure

Notify regulators where required, verify remediation, formally close the breach record

📜 Policy Review & Approval Lifecycle
1
Review Trigger

Policy owner initiates review (scheduled cycle, regulatory change, material incident, or gap identified)

2
Drafting & Revision

GRC team reviews, updates, documents all changes, and assigns a new version number

3
Stakeholder Consultation

Circulated to Legal, Compliance, and relevant functions (RACI — Consulted) for review and input

4
Formal Approval

Submitted to the Accountable authority (RACI — Accountable) for final approval and sign-off

5
Publication & Communication

Updated policy published, relevant parties notified, Policy Register POL updated accordingly

🔓 Exception Management & Risk Acceptance Workflow
1
Exception Request

Business unit submits a justified exception request from a specific policy, standard, or control

2
Risk Assessment

GRC Unit assesses risks arising from granting the exception and identifies compensating controls

3
Formal Risk Acceptance

Documented Risk Acceptance statement with clear decision authority and residual risk acknowledged

4
Approval & Documentation

Exception approved at the appropriate authority level and logged in the Exception Register

5
Periodic Review & Closure

Exception reviewed periodically for continued validity and automatically expires upon end date

RACI Matrix — Accountability & Responsibility Framework
Defining the true owner of each domain · R=Responsible, A=Accountable, C=Consulted, I=Informed
📌

Why is a RACI Matrix critical in Government GRC?

The absence of a RACI matrix leads to overlapping authorities, accountability gaps, and delayed risk responses. This matrix precisely defines who owns each decision, who approves, who is consulted, and who is informed — it is the operational backbone of the Three Lines Model under IIA Standards 2024.

International Standards — Detail & Government Application
Each standard linked to its domain and relevance to government entities
COSO

COSO ERM 2017

Committee of Sponsoring Organizations · United States
  • The most comprehensive enterprise risk management framework — integrating ERM with strategy and performance
  • Five components: Governance & Culture, Strategy & Objective-Setting, Performance, Review & Revision, Information & Communication
  • 20 detailed principles governing institutional-level risk management
  • Government application: linking budget and project risks to strategic direction
ISO

ISO 31000:2018

International Organization for Standardization — Global Risk Management Standard
  • The global gold standard for risk management — principles, framework, and integrated process
  • Core principles: integration, structured approach, customisation, inclusivity, dynamism, reliable information
  • Risk management cycle: communication, context, assessment, treatment, monitoring
  • Applicable to all risk types: operational, legal, financial, reputational
ISO

ISO 37301:2021

Compliance Management System — Successor to ISO 19600
  • The first international standard offering certification in compliance management systems
  • Covers: compliance culture, risk appetite, leadership commitment, performance evaluation, operational controls, continual improvement
  • Aligned with ISO High-Level Structure (HLS), enabling seamless integration with ISO 9001 and ISO 27001
  • Government application: demonstrating compliance to oversight bodies and achieving sound governance
COBIT

COBIT 2019

ISACA — IT Governance and Management Framework
  • The leading framework for enterprise IT governance and management
  • Two domains: Governance (EDM) and IT Management (APO, BAI, DSS, MEA) — 40 objectives
  • Capability Maturity Framework (CMF) enables assessment of IT governance maturity levels
  • Critical for government entities undergoing digital transformation and e-service delivery
KING

King IV™ Report 2016

IoDSA — South Africa · Most Comprehensive Governance Report
  • The most recent and comprehensive governance report — 17 principles and 76 specific practices
  • Integrated governance: linking financial performance with social, environmental, and ethical dimensions
  • Activates the "apply and explain" principle rather than superficial compliance
  • Government application: governance culture model and board/council leadership
IIA

IIA Standards 2024

The Institute of Internal Auditors — International Professional Standards
  • International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing — revised in 2024 for broader applicability
  • Three Lines Model — defines the role of internal audit within the GRC ecosystem
  • Audit objectives: providing assurance and adding value through assessment and advisory services
  • Requires direct reporting from audit leadership to the governing body (board/council)

GRC Maturity Model — Five Levels
Based on CMMI, OCEG, and GRCE adaptations for government entities
📊

Institutional GRC Maturity Levels

1
Initial
Informal, reactive practices dependent on specific individuals. No documented policies or standardised procedures.
2
Managed
Partial documentation and emerging policies. Practices are semi-regular but largely siloed and uncoordinated.
3
Defined
Documented and approved policies and procedures. Systematic management with partial integration across domains.
4
Measured
Clear KPIs and KRIs, continuous monitoring, periodic executive reporting, and benchmarking against standards.
5
Optimised
Continuous improvement driven by data and analytics. Embedded institutional culture. The international gold standard.

The Three Pillars of the GRC Model
Conceptual foundation — each pillar reinforces and integrates with the others
🏛️

Governance

The system of decisions, authorities, accountability, and oversight that directs the entity towards its strategic objectives with transparency and efficiency. Encompasses organisational structure, policies, roles, and internal control mechanisms.

OECD 2023King IV™ COBIT 2019INTOSAI GOV 9100
⚖️

Risk Management

The systematic process of identifying, assessing, treating, and monitoring risks that may impede the achievement of objectives. Includes: risk registers, heat maps, risk appetite statements, and data-driven response plans.

ISO 31000:2018COSO ERM 2017 IRMFERMA
✅

Compliance

Ensuring the entity adheres to all applicable regulations, policies, internal standards, and international requirements. Includes: compliance programmes, monitoring, auditing, reporting, and embedding a culture of institutional integrity.

ISO 37301:2021ISO 19600 FCPAGRECO
